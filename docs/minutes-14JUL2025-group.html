49.4 Soil Concept Assignment and Classification Strategy

49.4.1 2024 Data Analysis Progress

Patty Burns and Nora Pearson are leading the effort to assign preliminary concepts to all 2024 pedons (approximately 180 total). Key findings emerging from initial analysis:

Recurring Patterns Identified: - Glacial Fluvial Concepts: Silty, loamy cap over sand and gravel - River System Inclusions: Similar textures found in river system areas - Fine Loamy Des Moines Lobe Till: Consistent patterns in Hennepin County park systems - Cross-Concept Occurrences: Some concepts appearing across multiple geomorphic surfaces

49.4.2 Methodology for Concept Assignment

The team established a systematic approach:

  1. Field Assessment: Initial concept assignment during field work
  2. Office Review: Post-field verification using all available resources
  3. Documentation: Recording both primary concepts and inclusions when patterns cross geomorphic boundaries
  4. Quality Control: Recommendation for single-person consistency in classification

49.4.3 Randy’s Visit Preparation Strategy

Philosophy Adopted: Visit new sites within well-understood concept areas rather than returning to previously sampled locations or exploring completely unknown areas.

Target Concept Areas for Expert Review: - River system locations - Fine loamy areas
- Glaciofluvial environments - Estimated 6 total sites (morning and afternoon sessions)

Geographic Focus: Emphasis on cities proper where sufficient unsampled sites remain within known concept areas.

49.5 2025 Data Integration

49.5.1 Immediate Data Needs

Brianna Hayward volunteered to enhance data entry workflow by adding: - Geomorphic Surface Classification: Using physio_main designations from mapping layers - HTM Depth Documentation: Numerical values in centimeters (0 if absent)

49.5.2 Process Standardization

The team discussed implementing consistent approaches for: - Single-person classification: To ensure consistency across all pedons - Field vs. office determination: Balancing field observations with comprehensive office analysis - Documentation standards: Recording what is observed rather than what is expected based on map units

49.6 Laboratory Operations and Analysis

49.6.1 Current Lab Status and Capabilities

Ava McCune provided comprehensive updates on laboratory operations:

Equipment Status: - Laser Particle Size Analyzer: Repair scheduled for Tuesday, July 22nd - pXRF Analysis: Recently added to analysis suite - FTIR: Operational and ready for high-priority samples

49.6.2 Sample Processing Workflow

Current Priority System: 1. High-priority samples (exemplary concept profiles) processed first 2. Standard workflow: Ball milling → Loss on ignition → pH/EC → Laser diffraction → PXRF 3. FTIR analysis integrated after loss on ignition due to faster processing time

49.6.3 Artifact Documentation Update

Ava reported discovery of additional artifacts during course fragment washing that weren’t recorded on original field sheets. This affects: - Sites originally classified as “natural soils” - Potential modifier requirements for affected pedons - Need for systematic documentation in tracking systems

49.11 Strategic Considerations

49.11.1 Geographic Coverage Assessment

The team’s systematic approach to covering the Twin Cities metro area ensures comprehensive sampling across diverse urban soil conditions. The current south-to-east progression completes their circular sampling strategy while maintaining adequate site availability.

49.11.2 Concept Development Maturity

The emergence of consistent patterns across multiple geomorphic surfaces indicates the project is reaching sufficient sampling density to establish reliable soil concepts. The identification of cross-boundary occurrences suggests more complex soil-landscape relationships than initially anticipated.

49.11.3 Equipment and Capability Expansion

The addition of PXRF analysis represents a significant enhancement to the project’s analytical capabilities, enabling contamination assessment that complements traditional soil survey approaches while supporting broader research objectives.

49.11.4 Quality Control Evolution

The shift toward single-person classification consistency reflects lessons learned from multi-person approaches and demonstrates the project’s adaptive management in response to data quality considerations.
